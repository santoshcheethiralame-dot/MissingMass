## Scoop status

**Partial scoop found.** Li, Hays, Ramdas and Raghavan (2026-11-11),
*Controlling the probability of unknown unknowns in pre-deployment testing
of AI systems*, [2611.15835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15835).

What it does:
- Gives an anytime-valid upper confidence sequence on the probability that
  the next test shows a failure mode not yet seen. This is a sequential
  missing-mass (Good-Turing) problem.
- Lets a developer stop testing once the bound is below a threshold.
- Is shown on SWE-Chat coding-agent traces, with extensions for
  distribution shift and imperfect labels.

Where it overlaps with us:
- the "unseen failures in AI testing" framing (intro, related work);
- E13 and PH6, since both are coverage-based stopping rules.

What it does not cover:
- **Many runs of a tool.** It treats one stream of tests on one system.
  We look at K repeated runs of a discovery tool (incidence data).
- **Catcher dependence**, as in E08 and E10.
- **Identity errors.** In our setting the same failure can be matched
  wrongly across runs (E03, E05).
- **Planted-truth checks on real audit or SAE testbeds** (E02, E06, E08 to
  E10, E15).

So R01's trigger is met in part, for E13 and the framing, not for Phase 1
or the safety experiments.
